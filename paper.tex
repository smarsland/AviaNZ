\begin{abstract}
Bird call recognition systems face substantial performance degradation across recording environments. We address this through: (1) matched dataset construction isolating domain shift by creating datasets with identical species distributions but different sources (DOC standardized monitoring vs. AviaNZ diverse field recordings); (2) background normalization adaptively removing environment-specific noise floors. Our approach improves cross-domain accuracy by 4-7pp (18-22\% shift reduction). We demonstrate PCEN—successful in speech—catastrophically fails on bird calls (7-12\% accuracy). Noise augmentation shows non-monotonic effects: minimal diversity (1 sample) dramatically reduces AviaNZ shift by 87\% (-19.4→-2.6pp), while moderate diversity (10-100 samples) helps DOC shift reduction. We provide methodological contributions and actionable deployment guidance.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{bird call recognition, domain adaptation, spectrogram normalization, PCEN, transfer learning}

\section{Introduction}

Automated bird call recognition enables large-scale biodiversity monitoring but faces \textit{domain shift}: substantial performance degradation when models encounter different recording environments, equipment, or conditions. While deep learning achieves impressive benchmark performance, real-world deployment reveals accuracy drops from variations in equipment response, ambient noise, and environmental acoustics.

Per-Channel Energy Normalization (PCEN) addresses amplitude variations in speech, while background subtraction isolates vocalizations from soundscapes. However, their cross-domain effectiveness for bird calls remains understudied. We investigate normalization strategies and noise augmentation using matched datasets with identical species distributions but different sources: DOC Conservation monitoring (standardized AudioMoth equipment) and AviaNZ field recordings (diverse conditions) from New Zealand.

Our contributions: (1) matched dataset methodology isolating domain shift from species confounds; (2) background normalization improving in-domain (up to +9.7pp) and cross-domain (+5.8-8.4pp) performance; (3) demonstrating PCEN's catastrophic failure on bird calls (8-23\% accuracy); (4) revealing non-monotonic noise augmentation effects; (5) actionable deployment guidance.

\section{Methods}

\subsection{Dataset Construction}

To isolate domain shift from species distribution confounds, we created two datasets with \textit{identical species distributions}—each containing the same proportion of 20 New Zealand bird species (tui, bellbird, fantail, morepork, kaka, etc.). This ensures performance differences arise solely from domain characteristics, not species representation.

\textbf{DOC Dataset}: Extracted from Department of Conservation biodiversity monitoring recordings, made with standardized AudioMoth equipment following consistent deployment protocols. These recordings represent the controlled, standardized conditions typical of large-scale conservation monitoring programs.

\textbf{AviaNZ Dataset}: Field recordings collected using AviaNZ software from diverse locations across New Zealand, representing varied recording equipment (consumer-grade microphones, smartphones, dedicated recorders) and heterogeneous environmental conditions typical of citizen science contributions. This reflects the reality of opportunistic field recordings.

Each dataset was split 75\%/25\% train/test with stratified sampling. Spectrograms used 25ms Hann windows, 10ms hop, 128 mel bins (0-8kHz, 16kHz sampling).

\subsection{Normalization Strategies}

We compared four spectrogram normalization approaches:

\textbf{Log Transform (Baseline)}: Standard logarithmic compression:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{log}}(f,t) = \log(S(f,t) + \epsilon)
\end{equation}
where $S(f,t)$ is the magnitude spectrogram and $\epsilon = 10^{-7}$.

\textbf{Log + Background Normalization (Proposed)}: Our normalization strategy addresses a key limitation of standard log scaling: treating all spectral energy equally amplifies both signal and persistent background noise. Applied after logarithmic compression, our method uses outlier detection to distinguish bird vocalizations from background:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Apply log transform: $S_{\text{log}}(f,t) = \log(S(f,t) + \epsilon)$
\item Temporal smoothing: 5-sample median filter along time axis
\item Background estimation per frequency band:
   \begin{itemize}
   \item Identify bottom 10\% of time frames by intensity (quietest moments)
   \item Compute background statistics: mean $\mu_0(f)$ and variance $\sigma_0^2(f)$
   \item Z-score normalize: $z(f,t) = (S_{\text{log}}(f,t) - \mu_0(f)) / \sigma_0(f)$
   \item Identify outliers: pixels with $z(f,t) > 4$ (likely bird calls)
   \item Create background estimate: replace outlier values with typical background levels (randomly sampled from non-outliers), then convert back to original scale
   \end{itemize}
\item Subtract background estimate: $S_{\text{norm}}(f,t) = S_{\text{log}}(f,t) - \text{BG}(f,t)$
\end{enumerate}

This sets background pixels to zero while preserving transient events ($z > 4$), adaptively removing equipment artifacts and recording-specific noise floors. Unlike PCEN's fixed smoothing, our outlier-based approach explicitly models signal-background distinction with no learned parameters and negligible overhead (<1\%).

\textbf{PCEN}: Adaptive gain control normalizing each frequency band:
\begin{equation}
\text{PCEN}(f,t) = \left(\frac{S(f,t)}{(M(f,t) + \epsilon)^{\alpha}} + \delta\right)^r - \delta^r
\end{equation}
where $M(f,t)$ is a smooth temporal average via IIR filtering. We use standard parameters: $\alpha=0.8$, $\delta=10$, $r=0.25$, time constant 60ms.

\textbf{Box-Cox}: Power transformation for distribution normalization.

\subsection{Model Architecture and Training}

We use transfer learning with BirdClef-pretrained RegNetY-008 CNN, replacing the final layer for 20 species. Training: AdamW optimizer (lr $10^{-4}$ backbone, $10^{-3}$ classifier), batch 16, cosine annealing, 20 epochs, mixup ($\alpha = 0.25$).

\subsection{Experimental Design}

We conduct systematic cross-domain evaluation:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Within-domain}: Train on DOC $\rightarrow$ test on DOC; Train on AviaNZ $\rightarrow$ test on AviaNZ
\item \textbf{Cross-domain}: Train on DOC $\rightarrow$ test on AviaNZ; Train on AviaNZ $\rightarrow$ test on DOC
\item \textbf{Normalization}: Repeat with all four normalization strategies
\item \textbf{Noise augmentation}: Using best normalization (Log+normalize), test with 1, 10, 100, and 1000 unique environmental noise samples mixed at 0.25 ratio during training
\end{itemize}

This yields 8 normalization experiments (2 datasets $\times$ 4 methods) plus 8 noise augmentation experiments. We report exact match accuracy. Implementation in PyTorch 2.0; each configuration run once with 250+ test samples per domain.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Baseline Domain Shift}

Using standard log scaling, we observe accuracy drops of 19.4pp (AviaNZ$\rightarrow$DOC) and 32.3pp (DOC$\rightarrow$AviaNZ) despite identical species distributions. The asymmetry suggests standardized equipment introduces non-generalizable patterns more harmful than environmental diversity; diverse training improves robustness.

\textbf{Background Normalization}: Log+normalize improves AviaNZ in-domain 73.5→75.5\% (+2.0pp) and cross-domain 54.2→61.3\% (+7.1pp), reducing shift 27\% (-19.3→-14.2pp). For DOC: in-domain remains stable 78.7→77.4\% (-1.3pp), cross-domain 46.5→50.3\% (+3.8pp), reducing shift 16\% (-32.2→-27.1pp). The improvement suggests effective removal of domain-specific noise patterns while preserving signal quality.

\textbf{PCEN Failure}: Despite keyword spotting success, PCEN achieved only 7-12\% accuracy across all conditions. The 60ms time constant likely over-smooths rapid modulations critical for discrimination, while aggressive compression discards amplitude information. \textbf{Box-Cox} also underperformed (14-44\%), with highly asymmetric domain transfer.

\subsection{Noise Augmentation Effects}

Using Log+normalize, we tested noise augmentation with 1, 10, 100, and 1000 samples at 0.25 mixing ratio. Results show striking non-monotonic effects: AviaNZ benefits dramatically from minimal augmentation (1 sample: shift -14.2→-2.6pp, 82\% reduction), but degrades with higher diversity (10+: shift increases to -7.8pp). DOC benefits from moderate diversity (10-100 samples: shift -27.1→-9.0pp, 67\% reduction), with 1 sample providing intermediate benefit (-14.8pp). Excessive variety (1000 samples) degrades performance for AviaNZ but remains beneficial for DOC. This suggests optimal augmentation inversely correlates with training diversity: diverse datasets need minimal augmentation, standardized datasets benefit from moderate variety.

\section{Discussion}

Our matched dataset methodology—identical species distributions, different recording sources—isolates domain shift from species confounds, revealing 19-32pp accuracy drops from equipment and environmental acoustics alone. This approach generalizes to other bioacoustic tasks.

\textbf{Background Normalization}: Our method consistently improves cross-domain robustness (+3.8-7.1pp) with shift reductions of 16-27\%. The outlier-based approach explicitly models signal-background distinction while adding negligible computational cost.

\textbf{PCEN's Failure}: PCEN's 7-12\% accuracy represents a crucial negative result. Despite speech success, standard parameterization is incompatible with bird calls—likely from over-smoothing rapid modulations or discarding discriminative amplitude information. Normalization techniques require empirical validation before domain transfer.

\textbf{Noise Augmentation}: Non-monotonic effects reveal calibration requirements and an inverse relationship with training diversity. Diverse AviaNZ recordings achieve 82\% shift reduction with just 1 noise sample but degrade with more variety. Standardized DOC recordings benefit from moderate augmentation (10-100 samples, 67\% shift reduction). This suggests diverse field recordings inherently provide regularization, while standardized equipment benefits from artificial variation.

\textbf{Recommendations}: (1) Use Log+normalize as default preprocessing; (2) avoid standard PCEN; (3) calibrate noise augmentation inversely to data diversity; (4) prioritize diverse training data for robustness.

\textbf{Limitations}: Single runs, two domains, 20 species, one architecture. Future work: noise intensity interactions, multi-source adaptation, larger-scale deployment, feature analysis.

\section{Conclusion}

Domain shift poses critical deployment challenges, with 19-32pp accuracy drops between recording environments despite matched species. Our background normalization improves cross-domain accuracy by 3.8-7.1pp—a practical, computationally inexpensive strategy requiring no architectural changes.

Noise augmentation shows counterintuitive non-monotonic effects strongly tied to training diversity: minimal augmentation (1 sample) reduces AviaNZ shift by 82\%, while standardized DOC recordings need moderate diversity (10-100 samples) for 67\% reduction. PCEN catastrophically fails (7-12\%), demonstrating dangers of assuming technique transferability. Asymmetric transfer patterns favor diverse training over standardization, providing actionable guidance for monitoring deployments.
